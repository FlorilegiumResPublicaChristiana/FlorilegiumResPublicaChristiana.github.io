Teaching learning to read legal Latin with the sources

The platform is built to teach as well as to research. Three tools to learn with, and courses that use them: watch a seminar, read a charter with every word glossed, test your own translation against Mike's.

I

Seminar

Watch a translation being judged

Two models translate the same passage with the same glossary; a third judges them against the edition. Five passages from Roman law, canon law, political thought and a Transylvanian charter, with their real scores and mistakes.

  • Click the Latin for the glossary and the disputed phrases
  • Compare Mike and Gemma, error by error
  • See where the judge itself went wrong
Open seminar →II

Gloss reading

Read a charter word by word

A royal charter of 1220 laid out as on the platform: the Latin in its diplomatic parts, with notes in the margins, the key terms, people and places marked, and a dictionary behind every word.

  • Click any word for its grammar and dictionary entries
  • Follow the charter from invocation to witnesses
  • Open Mike's translation only when you need it
Open gloss reading →III

Test yourself

Translate, then compare with Mike

Four short passages of Roman law, from the definition of justice to the father's power. Write your own translation, then reveal Mike's and the edition's side by side.

  • Write your translation in the box
  • Show the key terms if you need a hint
  • Reveal and compare: yours, Mike's, the edition's
