Mike at work unaided drafts, and what they got wrong

Mike is a local language model being trained to read medieval legal Latin. These six passages show Mike's first drafts, made without help in November 2026, beside a translation reviewed by a person. They are a fair sample of the difficulty: Mike can often follow the sense of a sentence, but it can also reverse who did what or miss the legal meaning of a word.

That is why every translation used in the studies is checked, and why Mike needs better training. Donations help to fund it.

Béla, young king, 1231: Urkundenbuch I, no. 63 (Wenzel, ÁÚO XI, no. 164)

dum per divinae inspirationis gratia voluntate patris nostri et maturo principum terrae nostrae consilio inutiles et superfluas donationes, quae falso nomine perpetuitates nuncupantur, destrueremus

Mike's draft

While by the favor of divine inspiration and the will of our father and the mature counsel of the princes of our land, we destroy useless and superfluous donations, which are falsely called perpetuities.

What Mike got right

Right on the key phrase; the tense is loose, and its notes wrongly gloss falso as “forgery” and call the grants “fraudulent”.

Reviewed translation

while, by the grace of divine inspiration, by our father’s will and on the mature counsel of the magnates of our land, we were destroying useless and superfluous grants which are falsely called perpetuities

Judges, 1236: Wenzel, ÁÚO VII, no. 12

Robertus Dei gracia Strigoniensis Archiepiscopus, Sebes Comes Nitriensis, Andreas Ruphus, Zobozlaus, Micche, Zochyt Comites, et alij coniudices eorumdem, a domino Bela illustri Rege Hungarie ad revocandas perpetuitates in Strigoniensi Diocessi constituti

Mike's draft

Robertus, by the grace of God, Archbishop of Strigonium, Sebes, Count of Nyitra, Andreas Ruphus, Zobozlaus, Micche, Zochyt, the Counts, and other co-judges of theirs, were constituted by Lord Béla, the illustrious King of Hungary, to reinstate the perpetuities in the Diocese of Strigonium.

What went wrong

“To reinstate the perpetuities”: the opposite of ad revocandas.

Reviewed translation

Robert, by the grace of God archbishop of Esztergom, Sebes, count of Nitra, Andrew Rufus, Zobozlaus, Micche and Zochyt, counts, and their other fellow judges, appointed by the lord Béla, illustrious king of Hungary, to revoke the perpetuities in the diocese of Esztergom

Andrew II, 1232: Wenzel, Árpádkori új okmánytár VI, no. 320

cum karissimus primogenitus noster B. Rex in disscussione perpetuitatum terras quasdam, castrorum videlicet Albensis et Cenadiensis, Orodiensis et de Szarand, quas exceptas ab eisdem castris dilecto et fideli nostro Comiti Nicholao, fratri Vgrini, pro multiplicibus suis seruiciis contuleramus, ab eodem Comite abstulisset

Mike's draft

Since our most beloved firstborn son, King B., in the discussion of perpetual lands, namely the castles of Alba and Cenad, Orodi and Szarand, which were excepted from the same castles, had granted to our beloved and faithful count Nicholas, brother Ugrin, for his numerous services, but the same count had taken them away.

What went wrong

Reverses the action: says Béla granted the lands and the count seized them. Also “perpetual lands”, and “brother Ugrin”.

Reviewed translation

when our dearest firstborn, King B[éla], in the examination of the perpetuities had taken from that same count certain lands, namely of the castles of Alba, Cenad, Arad and Zarand, which, separated from those castles, we had conferred on our beloved and faithful Count Nicholas, brother of Ugrin, for his many services

Judges of perpetuities, 1234: Wenzel, ÁÚO I, no. 202

Vryas Dei gracia Abbas Sancti Martini de Monte Panuonie , et Bug et Baya , Judices perpetuitatum a domino Andrea Rege constituti salutem

Mike's draft

Vryas, by the grace of God, Abbot of Saint Martin of Mount Panuonie, and Bug and Baya, judges of perpetuity appointed by Lord Andrew the King, greetings.

What went wrong

“Judges of perpetuity”, explained as judges with lasting authority: they judged the grants called perpetuities. Pannonhalma not recognised.

Reviewed translation

Uros, by the grace of God abbot of St Martin of Pannonhalma, and Bug and Baya, judges of perpetuities appointed by the lord King Andrew, greeting.

Coloman, 1237: Wenzel, ÁÚO VII, no. 24

quod cum frater noster Bela Rex Illustrissimus Hungarie, et nos de communi consilio Regni vniuersas perpetuitates superflue et inutiliter factas reuocandas decreuissemus et reuocassemus ab omnibus ; specialiter eciam ab Abraam Comite filio Marcelli Comitis terras suas, si que fuerant ultra Drauam, et nos recepimus, preter terram Pukur cum suis pertinencijs, quam sibi et per eum suis heredibus perpetuo statuimus possidendam

Mike's draft

That, with our brother King Béla the Most Illustrious of Hungary, and we, by the common counsel of the kingdom, had decreed and had revoked all perpetual rights made excessively and uselessly, from all; especially from Abraham the Count, son of Count Marcellus, the lands which, if they were beyond the Danube, we received, except for the land of Pukur with its appurtenances, which we have established for him and through him for his heirs to hold forever.

What went wrong

“Beyond the Danube” for ultra Drauam; “perpetual rights” for perpetuitates.

Reviewed translation

that when our brother Béla, most illustrious king of Hungary, and we, by the common counsel of the kingdom, had decreed that all perpetuities superfluously and uselessly made be revoked, and had revoked them from all; in particular also from Count Abraham, son of Count Marcellus, his lands, if any were beyond the Drava, and we received them, except the land of Pukur with its appurtenances, which we have established for him and through him for his heirs to possess in perpetuity

Béla, young king, 1230: Wenzel, ÁÚO VI, no. 304

Cum inutiles et superfluas perpetuitatum donaciones de uoluntate patris nostri Illustris A. Regis in irritum reuocaremus, fideles nostri Franco et Stephanus filij Bertoldi Comitis obtulerunt nobis priuilegium patris nostri super quadam terra in Comitatu Supruniensi, uidelicet uilla Muthsa, super quo ad nostram fuerant citati presenciam. Nos igitur considerantes racionabilem donacionis causam

Mike's draft

Since we have revoked in vain the useless and superfluous perpetual donations made by the will of our illustrious father, King A., our faithful ones, Franco and Stephen, the sons of Count Bertold, have offered us the privilege of our father

What went wrong

In irritum revocare as “revoked in vain” (it means annul); Sopron as “Supra”; its notes take causa as “debt, guilt” from the briefing.

Reviewed translation

When, by the will of our father, the illustrious King A[ndrew], we were annulling useless and superfluous grants of perpetuities, our faithful men Franco and Stephen, sons of Count Bertold, presented to us a privilege of our father concerning a certain land in the county of Sopron, namely the village of Muthsa, on which they had been summoned before us.
